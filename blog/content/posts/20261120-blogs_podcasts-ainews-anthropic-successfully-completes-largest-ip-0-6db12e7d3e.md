## 来源摘要/节选

> As we previewed on our Know Before You IPO pod, Anthropic set its IPO price at $83 and today $ANT closed at $97.17, up 17% from the offering price (drifting -5% to $92.60 after the close). The $81.3B offering overtook SpaceX's June IPO as the largest in history.
>
> Dario and Daniela (Dario’s remarks, Founders’ letter) used the occasion to say Anthropic would pace training and deployment based on whether it could keep its systems safe and under human control.
>
> Their Class F shares carry 50.1% of voting power on most shareholder matters. Expect the lockup expiries to weigh on the stock price as about a trillion dollars of shares free up over the next year, while Anthropic has to keep the world’s top model...for now.

> Dario Amodei's IPO remarks (official position)
>
> Personal framing: Dario and Daniela lost their father to a disease that became curable a few years later. Dario cited CRISPR's decades-long path from discovery to treatment.
>
> Core thesis: Powerful AI working with humans "could compress a century of scientific and medical progress into a decade." He stressed that only early results exist so far.
>
> Named risks: Misuse by individuals or nation-states, economic disruption, and loss of control. Listed safety work includes bio/cyber safeguards, alignment training, and interpretability ("like an X-ray").
>
> Key commitment: "We will pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control. We will not bend to either business or political pressure."
>
> Strategic framing: The IPO "only strengthens our resolve." He restated the "race to the top," which should work "regardless of whether Anthropic is winning or losing at any given time" (@AnthropicAI).

> New voice isolation model from Inworld (Activity: 860): Inworld released an Apache-2.0 voice isolation model on Hugging Face, with a demo HF Space. It supports 48 kHz audio and ships two variants: a 64M offline/non-streaming model using 8s chunks and a 58M real-time model using 20ms chunks, trained on roughly 10k hours of audio. The post emphasizes it is an isolation model, not a speech enhancement/restoration system like Resemble Enhance; one commenter reported successful cleanup of a degraded voice recording when combining it with an NVIDIA restoration model. Commenters were interested in how it compares with NVIDIA’s recently released commercially friendly NVIDIA-NemotronLabs-RE-USE model for restoration/enhancement-style workflows. There was also practical enthusiasm around use cases such as removing background audience/laugh tracks and salvaging old personal recordings.

> A commenter linked NVIDIA NemotronLabs RE-USE, a recently released commercially friendly restoration/enhancement model on Hugging Face: https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-NemotronLabs-RE-USE. They raised the technical comparison question of how Inworld’s voice-isolation model performs against NVIDIA’s model on the same denoising/restoration tasks.
>
> One user shared a comparison of Inworld vs NVIDIA Nemotron RE-USE vs Sidon: https://youtu.be/pnaCz0dZf9I. They noted that the 1GB Sidon model "enhances the audio spectrum and elevates the voice" while running quickly on CPU, and claimed Sidon outperformed NVIDIA Nemotron RE-USE on difficult real-world recordings such as far-field smartphone voice memos captured in a noisy car.
>
> A technically relevant caveat was raised about evaluation data: the demo appears to use synthetic noise added on top, which may not reflect naturally degraded audio with muffling, reverberation, distance, and complex background noise. The commenter suggested practical post-processing strategies such as overlaying outputs from multiple models or retaining some background noise to preserve perceived authenticity.

> Kitten-TTS-2 Pico, Nano, Mini: SOTA super-tiny Apache 2.0 TTS, up to 16 languages (Pico is just 4 MB) (Activity: 471): KittenML released Kitten-TTS-2 code and weights under Apache 2.0, with three very small multilingual TTS checkpoints: Pico ~7M params / 4 MB / 8 languages, Nano ~17M / 8.8 MB / 8 languages, and Mini ~80M / 48 MB / 16 languages. The project claims Pico runs faster than real time on a single smartphone CPU thread, targeting Raspberry Pi/phone-class deployment; resources include GitHub, an HF demo, and HF weights for Pico, Nano, and Mini. Early testing feedback says English/German quality is good, but punctuation/prosody handling may be weak: it reportedly “ran everything together into a single sentence” and mostly ignored commas/full stops. One commenter asked whether voice cloning is supported; the post does not indicate cloning support.
>
> A user testing English and German reported generally good audio quality, but found the model nearly ignored punctuation: commas and full stops were mostly not reflected in prosody, causing text to be spoken as one continuous sentence. This suggests sentence-boundary handling or punctuation-to-prosody modeling may be a practical weakness despite the small model size.
